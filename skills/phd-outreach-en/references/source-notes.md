# Source notes and adaptations

## Source boundary

The method was distilled from one recruiting professor's explanation of doctoral outreach, covering goals, papers, email structure, research matching, attachments and follow-ups. The supplied material did not include a publicly verifiable speaker name or original link, so this repository does not claim university endorsement or attribution to an official policy. It distributes distilled guidance, prompts and an original email skeleton, not recordings or verbatim transcripts.

The source initially mentioned North America and Hong Kong, then mainly limited the writing advice to the US. Do not treat it as a policy for every university, discipline or region. Transcription errors in the Chinese phrase for outreach were normalised; missing or unclear example details were not reconstructed.

## Ideas retained

1. Generate interest in the CV and further conversation; trust develops in stages.
2. Keep the email short and its main facts easy to find. The source emphasised roughly a minute of reading.
3. State the institution, degree, country where needed, graduation date and possible PhD start time.
4. Show actual personal work, including writing, literature synthesis, analysis or experimental design. Paper counts do not substitute for contributions.
5. Describe research even without published papers; do not automatically apologise or devalue the applicant.
6. Keep the email a preview and the CV the place for details. Usually attach only the CV, unless instructions say otherwise.
7. Do not interpret silence as proof of poor ability. The professor suggested a short follow-up after about a week.

## Ideas used conditionally

| Source view | Treatment in this Skill |
| --- | --- |
| Institution and GPA are the main signals of ability | State the background; make GPA optional and do not equate prestige with research ability. |
| Four papers by a master's graduate may be a red flag | Examine real contributions; apply no paper-count penalty threshold. |
| A matching paragraph is unnecessary | Remove lengthy descriptions of the professor's work; allow one specific supported connection. |
| Always address the recipient as Professor | Use it for a known professor; respect specified titles. |
| Ask for forwarding to colleagues | Keep it optional. |
| Up to three follow-ups will not annoy the recipient | Treat it as one person's view, not a universal reaction or a three-email requirement. |
| Follow-ups make professors feel guilty and reply | Do not adopt this psychological assumption or use pressure as a strategy. |

## Added practical defaults

Three paragraphs and roughly 150–200 English words, an identifiable subject line, visible placeholders for missing facts, accurate manuscript status, and one draft with a few editing notes were added for reuse. They are not quotations or thresholds from the source professor.

The source contained no standard English email text, CV-writing method or Zoom interview method. The email skeleton was newly written from the principles; the latter two topics are outside this Skill's scope.
